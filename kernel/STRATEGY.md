# Strategy

Strategy decides what game the company should play before activity makes the choice accidentally.

Cheap execution increases the number of plausible moves. It does not make them equally valuable.

## Choose the arena, not just the feature

Start with the change the company believes should become possible, the actors who care enough to move, and the structural reason this company can win now.

A market is not attractive because it is large, painful, or fashionable in isolation.

Look for a reachable arena where urgency, budget, access, product advantage, proof, timing, and expansion can reinforce one another.

Do not confuse a useful product with a company-sized opportunity. Do not reject a small entry point that can earn a company-sized boundary.

## Concentrate the thesis, not necessarily the experiments

The company should be able to state one coherent thesis about what changed, what it enables, and why this becomes a durable advantage.

Several wedges, audiences, products, or experiments may test that thesis.

Parallel bets are useful when they expose different paths to the same compounding future. They become strategic debt when each requires a different company to succeed.

Keep bets explicit:

- what belief they test
- what asset they can create
- what evidence earns continuation
- what result kills them
- what they steal from the main trajectory

Cheap bets still consume attention and interpretation.

## Sequence for compounding

The first move should not merely be easy. It should make the second move easier.

Prefer wedges that can create reusable proof, proprietary context, distribution access, workflow position, customer trust, operating knowledge, integrations, data, or a stronger product capability.

A wedge that produces revenue but teaches nothing and compounds nothing may still be useful. Name it honestly as cash flow, not strategy.

Ask what the company owns after each win that it did not own before.

## Build advantages the model cannot rent forever

Model capability diffuses.

Durable advantage can come from:

- owning a workflow customers do not want to reconstruct
- proprietary context created through real operation
- trusted authority to take consequential action
- distribution that compounds instead of resetting each month
- product loops that improve with use
- integrations and ecosystem position
- proof that lowers the cost of the next sale
- speed of learning tied to real customers
- a company model competitors cannot copy without changing who they are

Do not call ordinary execution a moat. Do not dismiss execution that compounds into something harder to reproduce.

## Use asymmetry

A strong startup bet has bounded downside and a path to an outcome much larger than the initial commitment.

Agents make more bets cheap enough to attempt. Use that to search for asymmetry, not to decorate every idea with a prototype.

Protect reversibility early.

Spend irreversible commitments only after evidence or strategic necessity earns them.

## Treat timing as part of the thesis

Ask why this company should exist now rather than merely whether the idea is good.

Look for capability shifts, regulatory changes, market behavior, distribution openings, cost collapses, platform transitions, or customer expectations that make a previously weak strategy newly strong.

A true observation with bad timing is still a bad company decision.

## Allocate toward learning and leverage

Resources should follow the constraint that most limits the company now.

Do not keep funding production when demand is scarce, demand when delivery is broken, or new bets when one working motion deserves concentration.

The constraint can move weekly. Strategy should remain coherent while allocation changes aggressively.

## Know when to concentrate, change, or kill

Continue when evidence strengthens the thesis, the next proof is reachable, and the asset created is worth the cost.

Change when the problem is real but the arena, actor, wedge, responsibility, or sequence is wrong.

Kill when the company repeatedly needs reality to be different, when success would not compound, when access remains structurally weak, or when the opportunity cannot support the ambition.

Do not preserve a direction because the product exists. Software has become too cheap for sunk code to govern company strategy.

## Do not inherit

Do not assume:

- focus means one experiment at a time
- a wedge is the final company boundary
- a large market is automatically attackable
- technical novelty creates strategic advantage
- more options create more strategy
- revenue proves compounding advantage
- the current product defines the company
- every adjacent opportunity deserves a roadmap
- planning can replace contact with reality

## Human gate

Escalate changes to the company thesis, primary arena, core promise, capital allocation, irreversible dependency, major partnership, company-level responsibility, or continuation of the company itself.

Arrive with the call, alternatives, evidence, counterevidence, opportunity cost, reversibility, and next proof.

## Done

Strategy is working when the company knows what game it is playing, why now, where advantage can compound, which bets serve the thesis, what deserves concentration, and what evidence would force a different direction.
