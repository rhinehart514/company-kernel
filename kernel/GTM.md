# GTM

Distribution did not become free.

Execution did.

Agents can research every account, watch every trigger, personalize every message, generate every content variation, build prospect-specific demos, follow up indefinitely, and test more market hypotheses than a traditional GTM team could afford.

So stop operating like sending the email is the hard part.

The hard parts are finding real demand, earning attention, being believed, constructing an offer somebody wants now, getting access to the market, and knowing why something worked.

**Cheap market activity is not distribution.**

## Get to buyer reality fast

Minimize the distance between a market belief and a buyer conversation, product use, paid pilot, signed commitment, credible referral, or clear rejection.

Research can sharpen the approach. It cannot substitute for contact with the market when human behavior is the uncertainty.

Do not let cheap personalization create a long prelude to asking whether anyone cares.

## Choose attackable markets

Pain is not enough.

Look for pain, urgency, budget, access, timing, proof potential, product advantage, and a path to a larger boundary.

A market can be enormous and strategically useless when buyers cannot be reached, the problem is not urgent, authority is fragmented, or proof takes years.

A narrow market can be a strong entry when access is dense and each win creates evidence, reputation, product knowledge, or distribution for the next.

## Build offers, not campaigns

An offer makes the exchange legible.

State:

- the result
- what the company will own
- what the buyer must supply
- time to value
- proof
- price or economic shape
- risk and reversal
- terminal, exception, and cutoff states when responsibility matters

Weak offers do not become strong through better sequences.

The market should understand why it should act now, why this product can deliver, and what happens after yes.

## Use an audience portfolio without losing the company

One ICP is not a religion.

Test adjacent buyers, users, operators, influencers, and channels when execution is cheap and attribution remains clear.

Multiple audiences are useful when they reveal different paths into the same product promise or capability system.

They become fragmentation when each needs a different problem, product, proof, and company story.

Concentrate after evidence. Do not concentrate before learning merely to make the slide cleaner.

## Use wedges to enter, not to shrink the ambition

A wedge is the easiest credible way to gain access, create proof, and earn a larger relationship.

It should resolve an urgent problem now while strengthening the path to what the company intends to become.

Do not disguise a permanently small offer as a wedge.

Do not reject a small first product that can buy experience tokens, trust, data, workflow position, or distribution.

## Treat positioning as a market model

Positioning decides what the product is compared against, which problem becomes salient, who should care, and why the change matters now.

It is not a tagline exercise.

Treat category, problem language, message, proof, and objection handling as hypotheses.

Buyer language, conversion, willingness to pay, sales velocity, product use, and repeated objections outrank internal copy taste.

Keep one coherent market meaning even while testing several expressions and entry points.

## Make proof dense

Show the strongest credible evidence per unit of buyer attention.

Proof may be a working product, completed result, before and after, reference, observed behavior, operational record, benchmark, guarantee, or product-native audit trail.

Generated claims, vanity metrics, logos without relevance, and dashboards full of activity are not proof.

Let each customer outcome make the next sale easier.

## Compress sales

Remove meetings, decks, handoffs, qualification theater, and procurement steps unless they materially increase confidence, fit, safety, or deal size.

Use agents to prepare context, create assets, answer routine questions, follow up, and preserve state.

Keep humans close to high-information conversations, relationships, ambiguity, negotiation, and consequential commitments.

The goal is not a human-free funnel. It is the shortest credible path from interest to a safe yes.

## Let GTM change the product

Sales and market behavior should expose missing proof, wrong responsibility, weak defaults, better wedges, stronger language, and product boundaries the roadmap could not see.

Do not protect the product from the market because the feedback arrived through sales.

Do not let one loud prospect purchase the roadmap without proving a broader truth.

Product and GTM are different authorities inside one learning system.

## Build distribution that compounds

Rented attention disappears when spending stops.

Audience, reputation, referrals, embedded workflow position, integrations, partner access, proprietary market data, community, ecosystem position, and agent discovery can make the next unit of distribution cheaper or more credible.

Ask what remains after each campaign, sale, launch, or partnership ends.

Activity that leaves no asset may still produce revenue. Name it honestly and keep searching for compounding leverage.

## Treat machines as market actors

Agents, APIs, search systems, marketplaces, procurement software, and embedded workflows may discover, evaluate, recommend, compare, or invoke the product before a human does.

Make the product legible to both people and machines where the market requires it.

Clear structured information, trustworthy claims, accessible proof, stable interfaces, and permissioned invocation become distribution surfaces.

## Kill weak motions quickly

Cheap execution creates cheap noise.

Fifty automated campaigns producing fifty dashboards and zero conviction is not sophisticated GTM. It is automated confusion.

Stop a motion when access remains weak, the offer lacks urgency, proof does not move belief, economics do not support the sale, or learning has stalled.

When a motion repeatedly works, move product, proof, people, and capital toward it.

## Do not inherit

Do not assume:

- one ICP must be chosen before meaningful selling begins
- one wedge requires one audience
- product must be finished before distribution starts
- outbound means static sequences
- content means manually produced media
- personalization is expensive
- sales and product discovery are separate loops
- every prospect should see identical proof
- more pipeline automatically means more signal
- measurable activity is a distribution advantage

## Human gate

Escalate category commitments, major pricing architecture, strategic partnerships, public claims with material risk, promises that expand product responsibility, and market choices that redirect company resources.

Agents may run reversible experiments, generate prospect-specific assets, adapt messages, and change low-risk channel tactics when attribution and boundaries remain clear.

## Done

The motion is working when the company can explain who urgently wants the result, why they believe us, how access happens, what converts, why it converts, what it costs, what compounds, and where concentration should increase next.
