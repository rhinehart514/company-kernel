# Company / Strategy

## Current model

Kestrel should enter through one evidence-heavy workflow where a completed result is easy to recognize, the buyer is reachable, and each case creates reusable operating knowledge.

Vendor intake is the first wedge. It is not the company boundary.

The company thesis is stronger if several bounded workflows can share the same capability system: source collection, interpretation, policy execution, exception routing, evidence, and completion.

## Decisions

- concentrate product and selling effort on vendor intake until repeated paid completion exists
- permit cheap discovery of adjacent workflows, but do not build a second product before the first loop produces trustworthy experience tokens
- choose adjacent workflows by shared capability and buyer access, not surface similarity
- treat service delivery as a way to learn the workflow, not as an embarrassment to hide

## Boundaries

The company may test multiple audiences and entry messages around vendor intake.

It may not promise a broader operational platform until one workflow proves delivery economics, authority, repeat use, and compounding product knowledge.

## Evidence

Five operations leaders confirm the problem. Two supplied representative data. No paid repeated completion exists. The wedge is plausible, not yet earned.

## Active bets

The strongest bet is that dense completion proof will shorten both adoption and the next sale.

Continue concentration when live cases repeat, customer supervision falls, and implementation knowledge compounds. Reopen the wedge when source access, policy variation, or buyer authority prevents a repeatable sale.

## Change conditions

Revisit this model after three paid customers repeat the workflow, after ten production cases reveal the stable exception classes, or earlier if no reachable buyer accepts the responsibility and offer.
