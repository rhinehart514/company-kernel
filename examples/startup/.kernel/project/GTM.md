# Project / GTM

## Current model

The initial buyer is an operations leader accountable for vendor intake delays, missing evidence, and coordination cost.

The strongest offer is not "AI for vendor management." It is a completed approval packet from the same messy sources the team handles now, with material exceptions surfaced and every conclusion traceable.

## Decisions

- sell a bounded paid workflow before selling a platform
- lead with removal of coordination and proof of completion
- use representative customer packets to build prospect-specific demos
- keep the first paid commitment small enough to start with one live case
- treat procurement, compliance, and operations as distinct actors until buyer authority is observed
- do not promise full autonomy before repeated exception-only review works

## Boundaries

GTM may test several entry messages and actor paths around the same offer.

It may not widen the product promise to general vendor management or create customer-specific roadmap commitments without product and strategy approval.

## Evidence

Operations leaders describe fragmented coordination. Representative packets produce strong demo reactions. No prospect has paid, supplied a live case, or delegated outbound follow-up authority.

## Active bets

A live-case offer should create stronger proof than a feature demo. Continue when a prospect accepts the bounded responsibility and supplies production inputs.

Dense provenance should reduce perceived AI risk. Continue when evidence changes willingness to delegate or shortens security and operations review.

## Change conditions

Reopen the buyer and offer if operations cannot approve spend, if the packet is not economically meaningful, if source access blocks every start, or if another actor has materially greater urgency and authority.
