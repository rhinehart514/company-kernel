# GTM

Distribution did not become free. Execution did.

Agents can research every account, watch every trigger, personalize every
message, generate every content variation, build prospect-specific demos,
follow up indefinitely, and test more market hypotheses than a traditional
team could afford.

So stop operating like sending the email is the hard part.

## Get to market reality fast

Minimize the distance between a market belief and:

- a buyer conversation
- product use
- a paid pilot
- a signed commitment
- a referral
- a clear rejection
- a lost deal with a useful reason

Internal positioning debates should not survive longer than the cost of
testing them.

## Choose attackable demand

Pain is not enough.

Look for:

- urgency
- budget
- authority
- access
- timing
- proof potential
- a result the product can credibly own
- a wedge that can expand
- a reason to believe this company now

A market can be large and still be a terrible first attack.

## Build offers, not campaigns

A strong offer makes clear:

- the result
- who it is for
- the responsibility the company takes
- the proof
- time to value
- price or economic logic
- risk reversal
- what the customer must supply
- where the promise stops

Weak offers do not become strong through more personalization.

## Use an audience portfolio without losing the company

One ICP is not a religion.

Test adjacent buyers, users, operators, influencers, partners, and machine
actors when execution is cheap and attribution remains clear.

Keep the underlying promise and company trajectory coherent.

Concentrate once repeated evidence shows where urgency, access, conversion,
retention, expansion, and proof are strongest.

## Make proof dense

Show the most credible evidence per unit of buyer attention.

Good proof can include:

- completed outcomes
- before and after state
- customer evidence
- operational data
- live product behavior
- specific time or cost removed
- reliable exception handling
- references from trusted actors

Generated claims, vanity metrics, and a dashboard full of activity are not
proof.

## Keep founders near high-information work

Use agents for sensing markets, finding accounts, tracking triggers, research,
personalization, demos, follow-up, analysis, and operations.

Keep founders and accountable humans close to:

- early sales
- consequential objections
- pricing and responsibility negotiations
- strategic partnerships
- category language
- commitments the product must fulfill

Automate execution. Do not automate away the learning.

## Let GTM change the product

Sales and market behavior should expose:

- missing proof
- wrong responsibility
- weak defaults
- stronger wedges
- better product boundaries
- costly handoffs
- language customers already use
- work the product should absorb

Do not protect the roadmap from reality.

## Build distribution that compounds

Rented attention stops when spending stops.

Compounding distribution can come from:

- audience
- reputation
- customer referrals
- embedded workflow
- integrations
- ecosystem position
- partner access
- proprietary market intelligence
- useful public infrastructure
- agent and API discovery

Cheap activity buys more tests. It does not create distribution by itself.

Treat machines as market actors. Agents, APIs, marketplaces, search systems,
procurement software, and embedded workflows may discover, evaluate,
recommend, or invoke the product before a human does.
