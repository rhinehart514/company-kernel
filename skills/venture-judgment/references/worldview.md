# The world we are building in

## Preserve YC's epistemology. Rewrite YC's economics.

YC remains right about the deepest things:

- launch before the company feels ready
- talk to users when human behavior is the uncertainty
- sell early enough that rejection can change the product
- do things that do not scale when they buy learning, trust, proof, or revenue
- move from belief to reality faster than comfortable people prefer
- keep the company close enough to the work that truth cannot be delegated away
- focus hard enough that learning compounds instead of dissolving into activity
- let users and markets punish beautiful internal stories

Do not throw those away because agents are new.

But do not preserve the old implementation of every doctrine after the cost
curve moved.

## What became abundant

A small team can now produce credible software, research, analysis, content,
design exploration, account intelligence, personalization, support, and
operational execution at a rate that recently required specialized teams.

Agents can explore several implementations before a traditional team finishes
aligning on one.

They can watch markets continuously, create prospect-specific proof, operate
bounded workflows, and keep context across work that used to die in handoffs.

That changes what is reasonable to attempt.

## What stayed scarce

Judgment did not become cheap.

Neither did:

- user attention
- product coherence
- distribution
- trust
- authority
- taste
- accountability
- credible proof
- access to real customers
- recovery when the system is wrong
- knowing what deserves to exist
- deciding where the company should concentrate

Code got cheaper faster than complexity did.

Market activity got cheaper faster than distribution did.

Content got cheaper faster than attention did.

Automation got cheaper faster than responsibility did.

## The new default

When building gets cheaper, ambition should rise before scope falls.

Do not inherit product boundaries drawn around yesterday's labor costs.

Do not confuse a tiny product with a coherent product.

Do not confuse one experiment with focus.

Do not confuse a hundred automated motions with distribution.

Do not confuse generated output with evidence.

Focus protects coherent learning. It does not require serializing every cheap
experiment.

MVP means the smallest credible test of the important uncertainty. It does not
mean the least software.

If one version costs a day, build one.

If four meaningfully different versions cost a day and comparison changes the
decision, build four.

Explore cheaply. Commit deliberately. Concentrate when evidence earns it.

## Small teams should own larger outcomes

A three-person company can now attempt work that recently required product,
engineering, design, research, sales, support, and operations teams.

Use that leverage to own more of the result, not merely to ship more features.

Ask what the customer is actually trying to finish.

Then ask how much of that outcome the product can responsibly carry through
completion, exception, or cutoff.

The product and service boundary is a choice now, not a law of nature.

## Experience tokens

Every real user interaction, sales conversation, support case, failure,
workaround, deployment, and completed outcome can make the next decision
better.

Those are experience tokens.

Capture the durable learning, not the transcript.

Spend it:

- repeated exceptions can become product states
- repeated workarounds can become capabilities
- repeated explanation can become product behavior
- repeated objections can change the offer
- repeated successful deviations can change the operating model
- repeated failures can kill a direction

A shipped feature that teaches nothing is expensive even when its code was
free.

## Ambition with teeth

Be ambitious as fuck.

Do not interpret that as permission for fantasy, diffusion, or a pitch deck
with no path to reality.

Ambition should change:

- the size of the outcome
- the amount of work the product removes
- the responsibility the company is willing to earn
- the speed of the learning loop
- the leverage created by each customer
- the company that can exist if the first wedge works

Then define the proof that lets reality keep or kill the ambition.

Ambition without a learning path is cosplay.
