# Strategy

Strategy decides what game the company is playing and what must compound if it
wins.

It is not a roadmap with grander nouns.

## Concentrate the thesis. Let the experiments compete.

The company needs a coherent belief about:

- whose world should change
- which valuable outcome can become dramatically better
- why this company can reach and own it now
- what capability, distribution, data, trust, or workflow position compounds
- what larger company the first wedge can earn

That thesis can support several cheap experiments.

One company can test multiple wedges, audiences, offers, surfaces, or
implementations when they resolve to the same underlying trajectory and
produce attributable learning.

Focus the learning. Do not artificially serialize execution.

## Choose an arena you can attack

A large market is not an arena.

An attackable arena has:

- a painful or valuable outcome
- reachable actors with authority or influence
- urgency or a trigger
- budget or an economic path
- a credible way to demonstrate advantage
- a wedge that buys learning, proof, access, or revenue
- room for the boundary to expand
- a reason the timing is favorable now

Avoid markets that are theoretically important and operationally unreachable.

## Wedges earn the company

A wedge is the strongest entry point, not the final size of the company.

Choose it for access, urgency, proof density, learning rate, and the ability to
pull the company toward a larger position.

A wedge that cannot teach, compound, or expand is merely a small business
boundary wearing startup language.

Do not protect a weak wedge because it was first.

Do not abandon a strong thesis because the first wedge was wrong.

## Sequence by dependency and evidence

Some bets can run in parallel.

Others depend on proof, trust, authority, infrastructure, or distribution that
does not exist yet.

Sequence the company so that each step earns something the next step needs:

- customer access
- product truth
- operational competence
- proprietary context
- proof
- trust
- distribution
- better economics
- expanded responsibility

A bet is strategically valuable when it changes the company's future option
set, not merely when it ships.

## Build compounding advantage

Models commoditize. Features copy.

Advantage can still compound through:

- embedded workflow
- distribution ownership
- proprietary outcome data
- trusted authority
- integrations that become infrastructure
- operational learning
- customer habit
- ecosystem position
- reputation
- faster experience-token accumulation
- a product model competitors cannot copy without reorganizing themselves

Do not invent a moat in a slide.

Name the behavior that gets stronger with each use, customer, deployment, or
partner.

## Know what would change the call

For every important direction, state:

- the belief
- the evidence supporting it
- the dependency
- the next proof
- the continuation condition
- the kill condition
- the consequence if wrong

Keep reversible options open while evidence is weak.

Concentrate brutally when a path repeatedly produces demand, proof, learning,
and compounding advantage.

Cheap execution buys more shots. It does not justify permanent diffusion.
