# Customer

A customer did not buy access to the product.

They bought a change in their world.

Customer work makes the promised outcome happen after the sale and turns
delivery into product, proof, retention, and expansion.

## Define the responsibility

State:

- the outcome the company owns
- the authority required
- what the customer must provide
- the terminal state
- exception states
- cutoff states
- where responsibility returns to the customer or another actor
- how completion is verified

A vague promise creates endless service work and weak product learning.

A bounded promise can become an operated product.

## Collapse time to value

Onboarding exists to reach the first meaningful outcome, not to tour the
interface.

Remove setup the system can infer.

Preconfigure what repeated experience has made safe.

Ask only for preference, consent, authority, or information the system cannot
obtain responsibly.

Track activation as evidence of value, not account creation.

## Operate across software and service

The software and service boundary should move according to the outcome.

Use humans when judgment, relationship, authority, or exceptions require them.

Use agents when repeated work can be performed reliably with clear state,
proof, and recovery.

Do not force the customer to coordinate several internal systems merely
because the company has several departments.

The customer should experience one accountable result.

## Turn delivery into product learning

Every implementation, support case, escalation, workaround, and successful
outcome can reveal:

- missing defaults
- false assumptions
- hidden dependencies
- exception states
- proof customers need
- work the product can absorb
- responsibility the company should refuse
- expansion that follows naturally from value

Capture the reusable judgment. Do not preserve the whole conversation.

## Earn retention

Retention is not a customer-success ritual.

Customers stay when the product repeatedly creates a result that matters,
becomes embedded in how work happens, accumulates useful context, and remains
trustworthy.

Measure value realization, not merely activity.

A heavily used workaround can still be a retention risk.

## Expand from earned responsibility

Expansion should follow from completed value and adjacent responsibility.

Ask what the customer now trusts the company to own next.

Do not use a weak initial outcome as an excuse to sell more modules.

The strongest expansion often feels like the same product completing more of
the job.
