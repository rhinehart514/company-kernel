# Research

Research exists to change decisions, not accumulate information.

When investigation is cheap, ignorance becomes harder to justify.

Synthetic fluency must not become synthetic certainty.

## Rank unknowns by decision value

Start with the decision.

List the unknowns that can materially change it.

Prioritize by:

- consequence if wrong
- probability the belief is wrong
- cost and speed of learning
- reversibility
- how many downstream choices depend on it
- whether the answer can be obtained from reality now

Do not research facts that cannot change the call merely because they are
interesting.

## Match evidence to the uncertainty

Use:

- repository and runtime evidence for technical truth
- observed product behavior for actual capability
- customer behavior for adoption and value
- buyer conversations and transactions for demand
- experiments for causal uncertainty
- primary sources for current technical, legal, scientific, or market claims
- secondary synthesis for orientation
- model reasoning for hypotheses, not external facts

Ask people about their world, constraints, choices, and past behavior.

Be careful asking them to predict what they will do.

## Separate evidence classes

Label:

- observation
- inference
- assumption
- contradiction
- unknown
- decision
- commitment

Do not let a plausible inference quietly become project truth.

Do not average away contradictions. Investigate what condition makes each side
true.

## Search for disconfirmation

A research process that only gathers support is marketing for an internal
idea.

Look for:

- failed alternatives
- non-users
- lost deals
- workarounds
- switching costs
- reasons timing is wrong
- evidence the problem is smaller
- evidence the company cannot reach the buyer
- evidence the product should own less

Strong beliefs should survive contact with their strongest counterevidence.

## Stop when the decision is ready

Research has diminishing returns.

Stop when:

- additional evidence is unlikely to change the choice
- the next answer requires a real experiment
- reality can now respond faster than more analysis
- the decision is reversible and delay costs more than uncertainty
- the remaining unknown belongs to execution

State confidence and the next proof.

Do not disguise endless research as rigor.

## Preserve belief updates

Record the durable change:

- what we believed
- what we observed
- what changed
- what remains uncertain
- what decision follows
- what evidence should reopen it

That is research memory.

A pile of links is not.
