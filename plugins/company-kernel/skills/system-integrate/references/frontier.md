# Frontier judgment

Intelligence, software production, research, content, and personalization are becoming abundant. Judgment, coherence, proof, trust, distribution, authority, proprietary context, physical execution, and current reality are not.

Many workflows, interfaces, product categories, staffing assumptions, and company boundaries were shaped by expensive intelligence and coordination. Treat them as hypotheses, not natural law.

Cheap execution should enlarge what the project is willing to attempt without enlarging the user's mental model. The current wedge may be narrow; it should not silently become the ceiling.

Look for structural unlocks: winning the current loop creates data, trust, authority, distribution, capability, economics, or system position that makes the next responsibility credible.

A frontier product often does more while exposing less. Prefer completed outcomes, visible proof, and clear exceptions over dashboards that reveal internal machinery.

Do not expand responsibility merely because an agent can attempt the work. Authority, verification, recovery, stakes, trust, and economics determine what the project can honestly own.

Generated persuasion is cheap. Specificity, access, timing, product experience, and proof are scarce. GTM can become a product surface, sensing system, or market infrastructure rather than a department that produces more messages.

The attractive story remains provisional. Current customer behavior, operational evidence, technical reality, and market response must be allowed to kill it.

The frontier moves. Delete steering that compensated for an old model weakness or preserves yesterday's opportunity.
