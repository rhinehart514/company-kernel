# Research judgment

The useful question is not what happened in the world.

It is what changed enough that a previously sensible project belief, boundary, product, GTM motion, or operating choice may now be wrong.

## Evidence surfaces

Depending on the question, inspect:

- repository and actual product behavior
- customer behavior, calls, support, sales, retention, and operations
- current capability thresholds, reliability, latency, and cost
- market structure and why current workflows exist
- competitor behavior and new company forms
- regulation, standards, procurement, and trust constraints
- pricing, labor cost, margins, and unit economics
- distribution surfaces and market signals
- failed attempts and reasons incumbents remain strong

## Frontier questions

- What recently became practical?
- Which old constraint materially weakened?
- Which boundary exists only because intelligence, coordination, or software used to be expensive?
- Can the project own more of the outcome now?
- What still requires human judgment, authority, relationship, or physical action?
- Does a larger company become credible, or merely a larger feature list?
- Can capability increase while the user's mental model becomes simpler?

## Market questions

- What creates urgency?
- Who has authority and budget?
- What proof cuts through generated claims?
- Which wedge combines pain, reachability, proof, economics, and expansion?
- Can product, data, service, or infrastructure create distribution?
- Does every customer make the next customer easier to reach, trust, or serve?

## Contradiction

Actively seek:

- behavior that disagrees with interviews
- reliability below the required threshold
- trust or regulatory barriers
- bad economics hidden by automation language
- distribution resistance
- incumbent advantages
- user preference for existing human or workflow boundaries
- failed companies with a similar thesis

Ambition should be generous. Evidence should be impolite.

## Research shape

Use programmatic collection and filtering for bounded high-volume work.

Use adaptive direct investigation for surprising leads, source judgment, contradiction, and interpretation.

Cite or record primary evidence close to the dynamic claim.

Stop when more evidence will not change the decision or next test.
